# Model selection and fixed pilot

This document records the completed data/graph inspection and the proposed model comparison. No new model has been trained for this pilot, and the reserved test cohort has not been scored. The numerical results below are development measurements; model improvements remain hypotheses until compared.

## 1. One real record through the pipeline

We traced the query `graph neural networks hardware acceleration` through the existing ranker and the 150k historical graph. The trace is in [pipeline-trace-150k.json](../results/pipeline-trace-150k.json); it is an illustration, not a ranking-quality benchmark.

The top-ranked researcher was Chang-Jiun Chen. One supporting record was DBLP key `conf/vlsi-dat/ChenCM18`, *Acceleration of neural network model execution on embedded systems*, 2018, VLSI-DAT. We also independently streamed the raw DBLP archive and found this paper, with authors Chang-Jiun Chen, Kai-Chun Chen and May-chen Martin-Kuo; it appeared at parsed publication record 187,457 in that archive. The normalized source database and graph agree on its paper key, title, year and author identities.

The graph contains three author–paper, three author–venue and three coauthorship events supported by that paper, all dated 2018. For the top result, retrieval created 200 author candidates; its recorded normalized lexical feature was 0.730, dense cosine feature 0.576, title coverage 0.400 and hybrid feature 0.903. The existing trained model assigned a ranking score of about 0.773. These are different signals on different scales; the score is not a probability of expertise.

**Diagnosis:** the record supports work on neural-network acceleration. Its title does not establish work on *graph* neural networks. A valid citation proves the attributed publication, not the stronger relevance inference. This example makes topical specificity a concrete reranking problem; one example is not enough to estimate frequency.

Two additional traces exercise different fields:

|---|---|---|---:|

These show that the source publication, author ordering and sourced graph relationships agree in the two additional examples; they are illustrations rather than assessments of whether the top people are the best experts. Full records, features and edge provenance are in the [remote-sensing trace](../results/pipeline-trace-remote-sensing.json) and [expert-search trace](../results/pipeline-trace-expert-search.json).

The 150k source-order slice contains 115,780 conference records and 34,220 collection entries, with no journal articles. Its most frequent venues include IGARSS (33,679), WCNC (13,434), DAC (9,751) and SIGIR (7,863). More papers improved coverage in the earlier fixed-cohort comparison, but this slice remains heavily shaped by source order. An external, distinct slice or field is needed for generalization testing.

## 2. Fixed pilot and reserved cohort

The cohort is generated by `prepare-pilot` from the existing 150k dataset, using deterministic SHA-256 priorities and seed 42, without selecting queries for whether retrieval succeeds. The graph remains frozen through 2018. The old 50k source's paper keys and normalized titles are excluded from new validation and reserved test records.

| Role | Years | Count | Use |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Training pilot | 2019–2020 | 1,200 queries | Fit candidate models; may include records from the prior training source. |
| Novel validation pilot | 2021–2022 | 400 queries from 9,318 eligible | Compare choices, settings and ablations; not a final score. |
| Reserved later test | 2025–2026 | 5,588 queries | Freeze and use for final in-slice assessment after selection; do not inspect individual examples or scores during tuning. |

The parent dataset's test aggregate counts were previously observed when preparing the 150k dataset. The reserved records have not been scored here, and the original 50k's keys/titles are excluded, but this is still the same source-order corpus—not an independently collected or reviewer-supplied external test. MiniLM's pretraining-data overlap with DBLP cannot be ruled out. The cohort builder mechanically reads, checks and copies the labels; the training/validation inspection does not open or evaluate the reserved file. Dates, data lineage and SHA-256 hashes are saved in [the cohort manifest](../experiments/model-pilot-v1.manifest.json). The generated label files themselves are under ignored `data/processed/model-pilot-v1/`.

### What we measured before training

On the 1,200 training queries, 817 of 4,630 relevant author instances reached 200 hybrid candidates; 384 queries had both a retrieved positive and at least one contrast candidate and are usable by the current trainer. That is a small effective supervised sample, even though 240,000 candidate rows exist. Increasing model complexity without improving useful supervision can overfit.

On the 400 new validation queries, 682 of 1,711 relevant author instances had historical graph records (39.86%). Retrieval reached 199 relevant authors: 11.63% of all relevant authors, or 29.18% of relevant authors with history. Candidates use the existing 256-paper / 200-author budgets. These ratios have different denominators.

| Existing method, fixed 400 validation queries | nDCG@10 | MRR |
|---|---:|---:|
| Lexical | 0.02908 | 0.04384 |
| MiniLM dense | 0.02488 | 0.03869 |
| Hybrid | 0.02939 | 0.04832 |
| Existing trained baseline on expanded graph | **0.03815** | **0.06273** |

The baseline model was trained on the previous graph; this measures transfer to the expanded graph and new validation queries. It is not evidence of a newly trained model. Report and precise denominators: [model-pilot-v1-inspection.json](../results/model-pilot-v1-inspection.json). Labels are future-authorship proxies; non-authors are unjudged and may still be relevant experts.

## 3. Proposed model candidates

### Candidate A: hybrid retrieval → cross-encoder → trained graph-aware expert ranker

This is the most promising advanced architecture to **test**, not a guaranteed winner:

1. Use BM25 plus dense vectors to retrieve historical papers and aggregate them to researcher candidates. Tune candidate budgets on validation and always report first-stage recall.
2. For a bounded number of leading candidates, pass the research query and a few historical evidence-paper titles to a cross-encoder. It processes each query/paper pair jointly and may distinguish topical details missed by independent vectors. Start with a small pretrained baseline such as [`cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2`](https://huggingface.co/cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2); its prior training is not our own training and does not by itself meet the assignment's self-training requirement.
3. Aggregate evidence-paper scores to researcher-level features (for example, strongest and top-k scores), combine them with lexical/dense evidence and dated graph/recency features, and train an author reranker. A query-grouped learning-to-rank model such as LambdaMART is one candidate; the existing gradient-boosted classifier remains a required baseline. The [LightGBM LGBMRanker documentation](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pythonapi/lightgbm.LGBMRanker.html) describes query-grouped ranking and its LambdaRank objective.
4. Preserve paper and edge provenance. A neural similarity score is not itself an evidence-backed factual claim. Compare graph feature removal and cross-encoder removal separately, keeping the same candidate set when measuring reranking effects.

**Training caution:** a future paper's author is a weak positive at the author level. It does not prove every historical paper by that author matches the query. Similarly, a non-author may be a valid expert. Any query/paper fine-tuning labels are therefore weak, require careful construction and error inspection, and must not include the future target paper as input evidence. Consider robust multi-instance aggregation, sampling of both hard and easier unjudged contrasts, and conservative filtering; evaluate the *complete author-ranking pipeline*, not just a paper-pair classification score. [Sentence Transformers' retrieve-and-rerank guide](https://www.sbert.net/examples/sentence_transformer/applications/retrieve_rerank/README.html) explains the candidate-then-cross-encoder trade-off, and its [training guide](https://www.sbert.net/docs/cross_encoder/training_overview.html) discusses hard negatives and overfitting.

**Costs and risks:** running a transformer for each query/paper pair can be slow on CPU; a three-paper budget for 50 researchers means up to 150 pair evaluations. Compare bounded budgets and p50/p95 latency against the actual offline requirement. The pilot has only 384 usable training queries under current retrieval; a larger, properly selected training set or pretrained cross-encoder may be needed before fine-tuning. False negatives, popularity bias, overfitting, and pretraining overlap remain concerns. The existing installed environment does not include a cross-encoder training stack or LightGBM. They have not been downloaded, trained, benchmarked, pinned or integrated into this project.

### Candidate B: trained ranking objective over current retrieval and graph features

Train a query-grouped LambdaMART model over the same candidates and features, plus carefully designed topic-specific graph signals. It directly learns ordering from query groups and remains small and CPU-friendly compared with a transformer reranker. It does not by itself improve paper-level semantic understanding or recover missing candidates. Compare it against the current classifier, and compare training samples and output labels fairly; an objective change may not improve results with noisy labels.

### Candidate C: fine-tuned bi-encoder + hybrid retrieval + ranker

Adapt an embedding model so relevant historical evidence is retrieved more often, fuse it with lexical search, and train an author-level reranker. This directly targets the 11.63% all-author candidate recall. However, the frozen graph contains history for only 39.86% of validation relevant authors; no retriever can produce a person who is absent from its graph. Construct training pairs without future evidence or false certainty about historical paper relevance. Changes to encoder require a new index and compatible model serving path. Fine-tuning may be better suited to a GPU notebook, but any deployed model must work offline and fit the submission's model-size or reproducibility requirements.

### Candidate D: late interaction or graph neural networks

ColBERT-style late interaction retains token-level query/document matching with precomputed document representations; see the [original ColBERT paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832). It may improve fine-grained retrieval, but increases index size and implementation complexity. A GNN might use graph neighborhoods, but the graph must remain temporally valid, noisy edges and weak labels can mislead it, and neighborhood expansion could amplify popularity. Neither approach should be adopted without a fair graph/retrieval ablation and an offline, resource-feasible implementation.

## 4. Recommended comparison and success conditions

1. **Keep the existing system as the measured baseline.** Preserve the 400-query validation cohort, candidate budgets, labels, graph, evaluation code and existing model. Baseline rankings and metrics are recorded above.
2. **Improve first-stage coverage experimentally.** On training/development data, consider a broader training cohort, author profiles or bounded neighborhood expansion. Select budgets only on validation; never inject held-out positives into inference candidates. Report candidate recall all-authors and known-authors, plus runtime.
3. **Train a query-grouped ranker as a low-cost reference**, then evaluate it on exactly the same validation queries and candidate set as the existing model. This establishes whether a ranking objective improves the task before adding neural inference. It is a proposed experiment, not already implemented.
4. **Add an off-the-shelf cross-encoder scoring experiment** on the same candidates. Measure score aggregation, memory, model size, CPU latency and validation metrics. Compare no-cross-encoder and cross-encoder with the candidate set held fixed. Only then decide whether weak-label fine-tuning is justified and whether a local script, local notebook, or hosted GPU notebook is appropriate.
5. **Train/fine-tune the chosen model together**, with explicit data provenance, seeds, logs, model hashes, repeatable dependency versions and a portable offline inference path. Avoid opening the reserved test for model selection. User involvement and AI assistance should be accurately described in the eventual submission.
6. **Freeze decisions, then run the reserved cohort once** for in-slice evaluation. A separate unseen DBLP-style slice/field is still needed to assess generalization. Include nDCG@10, MRR, candidate and history coverage, scoped ablations, uncertainty, latency, memory, two failure analyses and safety tests. The assignment does not specify a numeric ranking target, and no architecture is guaranteed best.

The assignment allows pretrained model files totaling under 100 MB **or a script that reproduces them**. Before selecting a neural model, measure the actual quantized/exported size and license of every bundled model and the ability to run offline on a clean machine. GPU notebook availability and this laptop's GPU compute support have not been verified. Do not describe external training data as project-produced labels or assume the encoder's pretraining was temporally isolated from DBLP.

## 5. Reproduce the completed steps

From the repository root with prepared offline dependencies and raw/processed artifacts. Use new output paths; no command below trains a model or scores the reserved test.

```bash
PYTHONPATH=src .venv/bin/python -m hiresense.cli prepare-pilot \
  --dataset data/processed/phase1_150k/dataset \
  --graph-db data/processed/validation-150k/graph.sqlite3 \
  --exclude-source-db data/processed/phase1/source.sqlite3 \
  --out data/processed/model-pilot-repeat --train-queries 1200 \
  --validation-queries 400 --seed 42 --test-start-year 2025

PYTHONPATH=src .venv/bin/python scripts/inspect_pilot.py \
  --graph-db data/processed/validation-150k/graph.sqlite3 \
  --index data/indexes/validation-150k --dataset data/processed/model-pilot-repeat \
  --cache data/processed/model-pilot-repeat-cache --model models/expert_ranker.json \
  --out results/model-pilot-repeat-inspection.json

PYTHONPATH=src .venv/bin/python scripts/trace_pipeline.py \
  --source-db data/processed/phase1_150k/source.sqlite3 \
  --graph-db data/processed/validation-150k/graph.sqlite3 \
  --index data/indexes/validation-150k --model models/expert_ranker.json \
  --query 'graph neural networks hardware acceleration' \
  --out results/pipeline-trace-repeat.json
```

Feature caches bind to the graph, encoder, label hashes and candidate budgets. Review [the training workbook](model-training-workbook.md) before training: a baseline-compatible training command can point `--dataset` to `data/processed/model-pilot-v1` and `--cache` to `data/processed/model-pilot-v1-cache`, and use a **new** model filename. The current trainer opens only train and validation labels; the new model has not been trained during this setup.
